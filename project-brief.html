Write a brief for an Information Technology project.

This worksheet tests your ability to define a project clearly before building it. A strong brief explains the goal, users, requirements, constraints, and success criteria—in plain, unambiguous language.

Age group: 15 Time: 35–50 minutes Skill: Requirements writing Deliverable: 1-page brief

Teacher/Assessor Checklist

  • Students use specific, testable wording (avoid “easy” / “nice”).
  • Features are measurable (what it does, not how it looks).
  • Constraints are realistic (time, tools, privacy, access).
  • Deadline and success criteria are explicit.

Worksheet: Project Brief

Complete each section using the scenario. Write in clear sentences and bullet points.

Scenario

Your school wants a mobile app to help students manage homework and study schedules. The app should reduce missed deadlines and help students plan their week.

Assume the app will be designed first (prototype) and may be built later. Your brief should still be written like a real client document.

Tip: include what it is and who it helps (e.g., “StudySync — Planner for Students”).

Use: “Currently…, which causes…. This project will… so that…”.

Example user types: Year 10 students, teachers posting tasks, parents viewing deadlines (if allowed).

Avoid vague features like “modern design”. Prefer “push reminders 24h before due date”.

Good constraints sound real: “No student data stored without consent” / “Must work on low-end phones”.
